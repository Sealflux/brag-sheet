## Insight Questions(For My Recommenders)

### What are three adjectives you would use to describe yourself and why?

Loyal, Resourceful, and Curious

Loyal: I could listen to any of my friends rambling on and on for hours. I value my friends.
Resourceful: When I face an obstacle, a challenge, the first thing I do is not ask for help. The first thing I think of is what am I missing, what do I need to learn or know.
Curious: I don't wait for a class to teach me something I want to know. I've taught myself PCB design, embedded firmware, 3D modeling, and 3D printing because I wanted to build things. Most of the time I don't know what I'm doing. Through my projects, I gradually learn how to do things and progress.

### What is your intended college major? What are your career goals?
Intended Major: Electrical Engineering or Computer Engineering.
Maybe Minor: Computer Science
Career Goals: I want to create awesome things. Currently that means electronics and CAD. I love brainstorming cool ideas and working on them. Long-term, I want to create something remarkable.
### Describe your most significant extracurricular involvements. Elaborate on your participation in them and why they are important to you.
Science Bowl and Science Olympiad: I do these because they let me use what I learn in class outside of class. In Science Bowl I specialize in Chemistry. In Science Olympiad I've competed in several events. My best results are 2nd in Remote Sensing at Regionals 2026 and 3rd in Machines at the UMBC Invitational. These clubs matter to me because they're an outlet for STEM outside of school. A place where I can have fun using my brain rather than worrying about a class grade.

Kumon Teaching Assistant (Oct 2024 – Feb 2026): I helped manage learning plans for 100+ K-12 students. I even onboarded a new coworker.

Peer Tutoring (2023–Present): I've helped multiple students across several subjects since sophomore year. In 11th grade, I received the NHS Outstanding Mentorship Award. Seeing someone finally have it click after teaching them is so rewarding. 

Robotics Team (2023–2024): It was my first real taste of engineering. I learned how to communicate with a team and follow safety protocols. I shadowed a senior and saw how they operated and worked.

Personal Projects, projects where I learned and created at the same time: Desert Royale, DeepThought, NFC Business Card, Friendship Signal Bracelets, Tilt, RP2040 Dev Board, ESP32-C3 Dev Board. 

### Describe any major obstacles you have faced and how you overcame them. Think of academic, personal, family, or financial struggles. 
My parents immigrated from Vietnam when I was 3 or 4. I never received schooling in Vietnamese, so I can speak conversationally but can't read, write, or discuss complex topics with them. This meant I had to navigate college prep, financial aid, and academic decisions largely on my own. I figured it out the only way I could. The internet. Asking Google, watching YouTube videos. The internet was practically a second parent to me.
### Do you believe your transcript truly reflects your academic abilities or potential? Elaborate. 
My transcript reflects my willingness to take risks and my resilience, but it doesn't capture my creativity, my resourcefulness, or my ability to teach myself complex skills (like PCB design and embedded firmware) outside of a classroom. I was working at Kumon, competing in Science Bowl and Science Olympiad, and building hardware projects on the side. My GPA only shows one part of me.

## Mr. Bates
### Why have you chosen Mr. Bates to write a letter of recommendation for you? 
Mr. Bates has known me since 9th grade through Science Bowl and Science Olympiad, where he's the club sponsor. He's seen me compete, study, and collaborate with teammates across multiple years, not just in a classroom setting. He's seen my ups and downs. He also taught me Aerospace Engineering in 10th grade, so he's seen both my academic and extracurricular sides. He knows my work ethic, my curiosity, and how I handle pressure. I trust him to write about my character and growth, not just my grades.

### What is a lesson or unit in the class you enjoyed? Why?
The bottle rocket unit in Aerospace Engineering. The idea itself was awesome, launching a rocket into the sky. I 3D designed a custom nose cone for my rocket. My rocket actually hit the roof of a car in the parking lot which was quite the memory. It was the first time I saw my CAD work in action, flying into the sky.
### Describe a time in the class when you felt most proud. Remember times when you displayed leadership, intellectual vitality, discipline, maturity, humility, integrity, or initiative.
During the balsa wood glider unit, I went into the classroom's storage closet to grab my glider and accidentally stepped on one that was on the floor. It was my friend Jonah's. I was the only person in the closet. I could've kicked it into a corner and walked out. Nobody would've known it was me. Instead, I took a deep breath, picked it up, and brought it straight to Mr. Bates. "I broke this. I take full accountability," I told him. He didn't get angry or punish me. He just said he'd handle it and started repairing the wooden frame. That moment stuck with me because it would've been so easy to lie, and lying would've meant carrying the guilt around. Taking accountability was harder in the moment, but integrity was a value ingrained in me. Through my parents, and through my middle school's motto: Courtesy, Service, Integrity. I'd rather own a mistake than hide one.
### What is something your teacher likely doesn’t know about you?
Mr. Bates probably doesn't know how much I struggled with English and communication growing up. My parents immigrated from Vietnam when I was 3 or 4. I never went to school in Vietnam. I can speak it conversationally, but I can't read or write it, and I can't discuss complex topics with my parents. Things like college applications, financial aid, or even my own projects. I've had to navigate a lot of my academic and personal life independently because of that language barrier. It's shaped my resourcefulness and my empathy for people who have to bridge gaps on their own.
## Mr. Papadopoulos 
### Why have you chosen Mr. Papadopoulos to write a letter of recommendation for you?
Mr. Papadopoulos taught me AP World History, and he's someone who saw me engage with history creatively and intellectually. He also still shows off the 3D-printed katana I made for our Meiji Restoration presentation, even now, in 12th grade, when I eat lunch in his classroom. That tells me he values the effort and creativity I bring, not just the grade I earn. He knows me as a student who goes beyond the minimum and connects ideas across disciplines. I want a letter that captures how I connect engineering with humanities. 
### What is a lesson or unit in the class you enjoyed? Why?
The Meiji Restoration presentation in AP World History. Mr. Papadopoulos let us add a creative element to our history presentation, and my team chose Emperor Meiji and the Meiji Restoration. I 3D printed a katana as our creative element. It was memorable because it combined my engineering skills with historical analysis. I had to understand the cultural and symbolic significance of the katana in Meiji-era Japan to represent it meaningfully. It also showed me that history isn't just dates and names; it's material culture, technology, and human decisions. That project made history feel alive. 
### Describe a time in the class when you felt most proud. Remember times when you displayed leadership, intellectual vitality, discipline, maturity, humility, integrity, or initiative.
I felt most proud when Mr. Papadopoulos kept my 3D-printed katana and still shows it off to teachers and students. It wasn't just about the grade. It was about creating something that had lasting value to him and to the classroom. Plus it's a cool katana.
### What is something your teacher likely doesn’t know about you?
Mr. Papadopoulos knows me as a student who brings energy to history class. What he probably doesn't know is how much is going on under the surface. Junior year, I was juggling six AP exams, a job at Kumon, Science Bowl, Science Olympiad, and my own hardware projects. There were weeks where I was stressed. Weeks where I questioned my own efforts. I don't show that often. I'm always smiling and bringing others up. I'd rather show up prepared and keep the struggle to myself. He's seen the katana; he hasn't seen the moments I stand in front of the mirror doubting myself.
